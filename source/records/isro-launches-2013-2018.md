# ISRO launches while I was at Legend Technologies — Jan 2013 to Jan 2018

Every orbital launch and major test flight from Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota, during my
five years at Legend. I went to SHAR as a vendor guest more than once and watched vehicles lift off,
but I did not write down which. This table is here so I can.

Dates and payloads are from public records (Wikipedia's PSLV and GSLV launch lists, ISRO mission pages
and NASASpaceflight), checked on 2026-09-19. The right-hand columns are mine to fill; each answer that
lands becomes a caption, an archive entry or a line in a Writing post.

- **Attended?** — did I watch this one from SHAR.
- **Involved?** — did any hardware or tooling I worked on fly on it, and how do I know.

## Why this list exists

The Writing entry on the nose cones says plainly that neither Chandrayaan-1 (October 2008, four years
before I joined) nor the Mars Orbiter Mission could have carried cones from the tooling I led, because
that tooling came later. The first half of that stands. The second half was stated too broadly: **MOM
launched on 5 November 2013, ten months after I joined**, and Legend supplied more to ISRO than nose
cones. Whether something I worked on flew on it is a real question, and it is row 3.

## The five worth thinking hardest about

- **Row 3, PSLV-C25 / Mangalyaan.** I was ten months into the job. I remember doing some assembly or
  structure work connected to it. If that is right, it belongs on the site — and it is the claim the
  nose-cone post currently declines to make on my behalf.
- **Row 17, RLV-TD.** The strongest documented link of the lot: the assembly jig is in the capability
  deck and its photographs are in the hub gallery. Did I work on that jig, and was I at the launch?
- **Row 25, GSLV Mk III-D1.** The deck shows a Mk III ½M assembly jig. Same two questions.
- **Row 23, PSLV-C37.** The famous one. Easy to remember whether I was there.
- **Row 28, PSLV-C40.** Twelve days into my last month. Probably not, but worth a line either way.
