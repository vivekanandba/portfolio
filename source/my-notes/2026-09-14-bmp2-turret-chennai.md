# The BMP-II turret in Chennai, and what the early years cost — 2014–2015

## On Gadjoy, nights and weekends

Late nights and the weekends, in Gadjoy — something that I wouldn't have told you, but that is
entirely true. During my primitive years — during the years that anyone in my age group would have
enjoyed by going out — I had to take care of a business that I started, over nights and weekends. You
are absolutely right: I couldn't go for any of the outings that I always wanted to go to. And this was
when I was what, 25, 26 years old.

## The BMP-II turret, and going to Chennai alone

Before that, even the BMP-II turret project I had taken up when I was maybe 24, 25. I went to Chennai
and I created — I went alone. I bid for the project, I won the project, and I went alone over there. I
created a team of engineers over there itself, and we delivered the project in three months. So I've
been doing this for quite a while.

That was one big push for me when it came to taking a project on my own: I bid for it, so I was made
responsible to complete it as well. That, during those initial years, gave me a lot of sense of
ownership. I had to even learn sales — how to go sell the company, sell our competence.

I was made responsible to go to Chennai. I figured out a way to go to CAD centres; there was a
training centre called CADD Centre, which I had earlier taken training in, so I thought I could get
good engineers from there. I made multiple things there. Instead of — we had a very limited supply of
workstations back then, so again I found out new ways of getting the workstations over there itself,
by having different contacts, finding it online. I think these were done in the 2014–15 range.

On the technical front, the drawings that we had to convert were like a sari. It was four A0 drawings
which were — like, within you, you try to absorb from one side to another side, and you would have
forgotten what were the details that were there on one side. So those complex drawings were there, and
a lot of people didn't have confidence. But I alone had gone over there. I gave confidence to the
customer, and then I myself led the team over there to convert it.

I had created a small org itself over there, wherein I was the lead integrator, and I had taken up some
of the hard assemblies to convert; the remaining systems were done by other engineers. And I had a set
of QA folks also in that setup itself, where they were responsible for doing the QA activities. So I
had set up a mini org after going over there — in a vendor's place, in a new city, in a place where I
didn't know who's who. But I had gone there, I had started the project, I gave the handover of the
project, and then I had come back to Bangalore. And that was when I was probably 23 or 24 years old,
which again gave a lot of impetus to me.

During the project itself, the technical difficulties — one is the four A0 sheets that I told you
about, but all these were very old Russian designs. They had so many layers of understanding behind
them, which was not very clear unless you started working on some of these assemblies or parts in one
direction. Once you start doing that is when suddenly you understand that there are other layers to it.
For example, I was just checking the simulations, since you had uploaded them, and I could quickly get
a flash of what were the difficulties that went through it.

I had to upskill. I was already good enough as an engineer, as a CAD engineer, as a designer, but I had
to quickly upskill my skills to see how to showcase what we have done — the complexities that we have
done. There were so many drawings that were converted; I had to quickly showcase this capability.

This was not warranted, not necessary in the project scope. The project scope was only to convert all
these 2D drawings to 3D models, assemble them, and then keep it for any other modification that BEL was
planning to do. But what we gave as an added value addition — just to show that the parts were done
with very high accuracy — is we had even animated them. We had made sure that it goes through the
process of how exactly the parts move. That aspect of what I did was a huge value addition, because
right now also I'm able to showcase the complexity of the parts that were there, and at the same time
we're even able to showcase how it is done.

And back then itself I'd created trackers to have project management, since I had to handle close to
around five to seven engineers. There was some work that was outsourced to Bangalore, so I had to even
handle the designers — the modellers — who were there in Bangalore as well. There were models that were
coming from here too.

Overall, a very good learning project during my very initial years — again, which would have been very
important for the entrepreneurial journey that I am in already now.

There are a lot of stories. There is a story behind each project, which is typically not captured — and
if it's not captured, I may also forget it after a few years.
