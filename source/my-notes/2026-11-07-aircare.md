# AirCare — two wires, a trade, six months stuck, the sensors nobody supported

_Spoken to the assistant; transcribed with filler words removed and punctuation added. Editorial
clarifications in square brackets. The mentor is unnamed here, as in the note of 3 October 2026, until
he is asked. Points that differed from earlier records were marked and settled on reading, the same day; his
answers are at the end. The industrial site he named is withheld: he could not point to a public
record that the readings were used against it, and asked that it be described, not named._

## Two wires

AirCare — my mentor had come to me asking me to solder two wires for it. That's how it got started.
When I was asked to solder those two wires, I found an opportunity: I had met an industry veteran in
the field that I wanted to get into. So I told him this: I will help you in hardware; please teach me
software. And that's how all this began.

From that time onwards I started to go to him once every month. I used to go and sit with him. He used
to give me just one hour [corrected on reading: half an hour a month, as in the note of 3 October 2026],
but he used to give plenty of information, an enormous amount. Most of it used to go above my head, but
I diligently went there, sat, and just immersed myself in the world that he was patiently letting me
know.

## The hardware I could help with

Slowly I started to see how to maintain, or to assemble, the AirCare unit. I helped him find better
alternatives, or easier ways of doing something, or lower-cost ways of getting the same problem sorted.
I could get Raspberry Pis at a much lower cost than he would probably have got them, because I was in
the electronics business myself [Gadjoy] and knew people who could give me a much more discounted rate.
Overall this was one big opportunity that I got to get into the field.

## Six months with my head down

But I really struggled. He gave me the codebase and asked me to make some small changes in the web app
he had built. It took me a long time, and it was a very hard hurdle back then, because I was just
beginning to understand software. I got into tutorial hell: I would go and see the code, not be able to
understand it, then go back and say I need to understand this, so I should take courses; then try to
complete the courses, and the courses took time again because of the things I was working on back then.
I used to go to him every month — I think for six months I went to him with my head down, saying I just
was not able to complete it.

Eventually I was able to break through. I started to slowly understand how to log in to the system — the
Linux part of things, the terminal, the whole way of getting things done on a CLI. I learned a lot
there. This was my initiation into software, and the first project I had taken up that I was involved
in.

## In the field

It slowly evolved into something where we were going and setting units up, or people were bringing them
back and we were rectifying them. The sensor was a Chinese one that we had opened up, so we had no
support for it here, but we were trying to see whether we could repair some of them. Importantly, we
found the ways in which people used to keep the unit that made it stop working, so we found ways of
making it work — right from cleaning it to debugging it. Everything related to the Raspberry Pi was
something I could very easily understand and get started on.

## The plant nearby

With the AirCare data near [a polluting industrial plant in Kundalahalli — named in the account,
withheld], we were able to provide very real evidence of the amount of particulate matter being emitted
from the industry there. I think that was one of the things used in the case against [it], and it was
probably taken down a notch after that. [On reading he agreed this cannot be stated without a public
record, and that the closure he had in mind was around 2017; the post says only that a
node sat near a polluting plant.]

All this came out of a community-led project: I could meet my mentor, and I could do a lot.

## Stop Hunger

During the pandemic is where I could find sufficient time to actually sit down and make a project work
completely, for Stop Hunger. We used Google Sheets as the database and a simple React app on the front
end, and we were able to get things done. We were able to support him with some ML-related work: I think
it was an inverse of a travelling-salesman problem, but what we had in mind was to figure out the
shortest path between all the settlements we wanted to provide food for. [See the answers below.]

We made very good progress on Stop Hunger too, and it was really something my mentor recognised, saying:
okay, now I think you're ready. That was the end of May [2020], and then he went missing. In November
2020 he called me and said he had joined a startup — and that's the journey to NovaSignal.

## Answers on reading, 2026-11-07

Sorry, it was half an hour per month. Whatever the site says would be the correct one — January 2018.
[The closure he had in mind was] 2017, yes. That's why you can say that a polluting company was near one
of the AirCare units. [He mentioned that the node was hosted at a clinic nearby, visible on the AirCare
map; the host is not named here.]

Since the sensor had to be exposed to the air, they had to keep it outside, and a lot of times pests used
to just go in, because it used to be put into a container. That is the reasoning behind it.

I think the first change he asked me to do was in the Bokeh blocks [Bokeh, the Python plotting library].
He told me I had to change the Bokeh blocks to showcase an aggregation, which I didn't understand in the
beginning. He had asked me to create Bokeh plots to visualise the day of the week, the month of the year,
and such plots. That was something I couldn't do.

Stop Hunger: yes, a route planner. I don't know if they actually used the route planner, but we did do it.
I used Jupyter notebooks. I was very interested in it, because we had the data as latitude and longitude,
and I was trying to cluster all these points together, and in combination with the clustering, see if we
could get the routing sorted out properly.

AirCare was built in '18 to '19, or he was doing work earlier than that. I was probably involved in it
from 2018 to 2019.
