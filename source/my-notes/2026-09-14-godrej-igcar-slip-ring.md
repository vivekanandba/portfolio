# The Godrej / IGCAR high-amperage slip ring — 2015

_Spoken to the assistant on the same day as the BMP-II note, after sending photographs of the NAMS
2015 proceedings. Transcribed with filler words removed and punctuation added; editorial
clarifications in square brackets. A third party's name is withheld pending his consent, following
the convention used for the deck transcripts._

## Winning it

In the Godrej slip ring I was involved in again the same end-to-end process. We had bid for the
project. Since we had slip-ring expertise, and we had ToT — transfer of technology — from ISRO itself
on slip rings, we were one of the internal vendors that Godrej had selected. Again here I had to
travel to Godrej Vikroli [Mumbai], and at the end of doing this project, hand it over to the customer.

It is used in electro-refining of nuclear materials, which is why the high amperage was very
necessary.

## Why it was a deviation

Once the project was given — we used to always do slip rings which were not with this high amperage.
It used to be mostly things that were very low amperage. We had used gold-plated beryllium wires so
that we have very little [contact resistance and wear]. We did the sizing, and since it was high
amperage there was going to be an enormous amount of heat getting generated. But the good thing was,
this didn't have high RPM. It had very low RPM.

So based on low RPM, high amperage was a huge deviation from whatever the existing ToT was. We had to
develop new ways of transmitting the electricity — very high amperage electricity — from the rotary
reference to the stationary reference. This was majorly for stirring; on top of the stirrer they had
this slip ring.

For this I consulted my own principal in my college. He had retired by then — [name withheld pending
his consent]. I consulted him through the company itself, for the design side of things.

## Production, and everything production taught me

Once the design was finalised we started production. In production we faced a lot of difficulties, and
a lot of understanding. Since this was electrical, we understood the aspect about having insulation
resistance between the different rings: each one of those rings had to have separate, or thick enough,
insulation resistance that the current doesn't jump from one ring to another ring. The enclosure, the
materials used for the enclosure, the materials used for the main shaft, the copper wires — we had
thick copper wires that were bent.

I learnt about wire EDM — electro-discharge machining — for some of these parts which were very small
and intricate and couldn't have been machined normally. I did electro-discharge machining for some of
these, as it was very difficult to get normal machining on them.

It was a stainless steel shaft, and we had to do a keyhole to connect the shaft, so that we can put in
a key to connect the shaft alongside whatever was there in the hollow shaft, so that the transmission
between this slip ring and the existing shaft could happen.

The holes for the wires were another problem. The wires themselves had to be able to transmit high
amperage without transferring it over to the wires beside — induction, I guess. We didn't want current
to induce into the other wires. So for that also we went and searched for different materials,
different insulation materials, different wires that could carry this kind of high amperage, in
Bangalore, Chickpet.

All those are very vivid memories: not just designing, which is again equally difficult, but all the
other things, just to make sure that we get the product right. Learning about different processes,
writing these processes, and making sure that the production people produce this to these
specifications.

## Testing, and a month or two in Mumbai

Once the product was ready over here we even tested it in an MSME lab. We did thorough testing before
taking it and going to Mumbai.

Once in Mumbai, Vikroli, there also, once integration was happening I was there for close to a month
or two — I'm not able to recall. I sat out there and we were involved in integrating this into their
use case, because I think that was supposed to be for a nuclear reactor that would go to Kalpakkam.
[Uncertain — his recollection, not a record.] So it was quite a journey of trying to get this done and
completed, in again a new city, a new place, new people.

There also in Mumbai I had to go and modify some of the processes to make sure that it accommodates,
so I worked along with the Godrej engineers to get the system integrated. As this was a prototype we
had a lot of back and forth with the Godrej team to make it more reliable. Rather than having a thick
rod, which we started to see in real use case was wearing, we converted some of the rod design into
fibres — and each one of those individual fibres was created with some amount of stiffness to it. I
found a workshop somewhere in Mumbai to get that done. Again there also I went shopping for beryllium
copper wires in Mumbai's [industrial supply street — name not recalled].

## The paper

It was just a vast level of experience, to understand the slip ring as a product itself — that we were
even able to write this NAMS report. When people see the NAMS paper written as publications they
should be able to click and then view this. You can see in the index I had published this paper
alongside the kind of projects that engineers from ISRO were publishing. It was a prestigious book or
publication that I was allowed to publish in when I was very, very young — probably 24, 25 at that
time. And I even went to Trivandrum, VSSC, to present this as a paper over there also.
